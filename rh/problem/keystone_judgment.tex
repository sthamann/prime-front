\subsection{(a) The smooth cutoff}\label{sec:a}

The proposal reads the $1/t$ decay of $\hat u$ and the size
$|u(\pm L)|\sim3\!-\!18$ as consequences of the sharp band
indicator $\mathbf 1_{|t|<t_c}$.  They are not.
For any $u\in L^2[-L,L]$ with $u(\pm L)\neq0$ the transform of
the \emph{restriction} decays like $1/t$; this is forced by the
compact time support and by nothing else.  No symbol smoothing
removes it.
Moreover the enemy symbol
$|\sigma_A|\mathbf 1_{|t|<t_c}=\max\{-\sigma_A,0\}$
is continuous with a corner at $\pm t_c$ (a derivative jump,
not a value jump), so its kernel already decays like
$|x-x'|^{-2}$; an upward mollification over a window $w$ costs
only $\approx|\sigma_A'(t_c)|w^2/2\cdot(2L/2\pi)$ in the enemy
trace.  Cheap, and irrelevant to a $10^3$ deficit.
\textbf{Kill as stated.}
The smoothing is nevertheless the right move --- in the
\emph{other} variable.  Smoothing in $x$ is Loewner-safe and is
part of (f); that is where the proposal is redeemed.

\subsection{(b) Trace powers}\label{sec:b}

Two independent kills.

\emph{(i) With the shift the operator has no Schatten class.}
The r488 test object is
$K=B^{1/2}R_s^{-1}B^{1/2}$ with $B=A^-+sI\succeq sI$ and
$R_s=A^++\Pi+sI$.
Since $\sigma_A(t)=\log(t/2\pi)+O(t^{-2})$, the Landau/Weyl
count for the compression of $\sigma_+(D)$ to an interval of
length $2L$ is
$N(\lambda)\approx(2L/2\pi)\,|\{t:\sigma_A(t)<\lambda\}|
=4Le^{\lambda}$, i.e.\ $\lambda_n(R)\asymp\log(n/4L)$.
Hence $\lambda_n(K)\asymp s/\log n$ and
$\sum_n\lambda_n(K)^m=\infty$ for \emph{every} $m$:
$(\operatorname{tr}K^m)^{1/m}=\infty$.
The route cannot start in the shifted formulation.

\emph{(ii) Without the shift the traces are not explicit.}
Unshifted, $K=(A^-)^{1/2}R^{-1}(A^-)^{1/2}$ is trace class
($\operatorname{tr}A^-=(2L/2\pi)\int_{|t|<t_c}|\sigma_A|<\infty$
and $R$ is coercive, \S\ref{sec:coerc}), so the Fredholm
inequality $\lambda_{\max}\le(\operatorname{tr}K^m)^{1/m}$ is
available in principle.  But
$\operatorname{tr}K^m=\operatorname{tr}\big((A^-R^{-1})^m\big)$
contains $R^{-1}=(A^++\Pi)^{-1}$ on $L^2[-L,L]$ $m$ times, and
that is a finite-interval Wiener--Hopf inverse: precisely the
object the whole chain failed to produce.  The premise
``$2m$-fold integrals of explicit kernels, no mode
representation needed'' is false here.
A Loewner minorant $R_0\preceq R$ with explicit inverse would
repair it, but no such $R_0$ is available for the compression
of a multiplier to an interval.

\begin{remark}
The premise of (b) is nevertheless correct --- in the
$x$-coordinate.  There the kernels \emph{are} explicit and
elementary.  That is a second, independent pointer to (f).
\end{remark}

\subsection{(c) The smoothing preconditioner}\label{sec:c}

The shift is used through the identity
$\QW=R-A^-=(R+S)-(A^-+S)$, valid for any $S\succeq0$;
a smoothing $S=c(1+|D|)^\alpha$ therefore lands on the
\emph{enemy} as well, and an unbounded enemy destroys the
finite-enemy representation, which is the one asset of
r486.
The alternative is a congruence: $\QW\succeq0$ iff
$Z^*\QW Z\succeq0$ for injective $Z$ with form-dense range.
Every Fourier multiplier $Z$ violates
$\operatorname{supp}\subset[-L,L]$, which is the defining
constraint of the problem.  The only support-preserving
congruence is multiplication by a weight $w(x)$, and there the
scaling is explicit and adverse: with $w\ge\varepsilon$ at the
endpoints the transform tail improves by $\varepsilon$ while
the margin degrades by $\varepsilon^2$, so the
tail-to-margin ratio degrades like $1/\varepsilon$.
\textbf{Kill.}

\subsection{The form}

\begin{lemma}[$x$-space form of $\QW$ for $2L<\log2$]
\label{lem:xform}
For real $h\in L^2[-L,L]$ with $g=h*\widetilde h$ and
$2L<\log2$,
\[
  \QW(h)=\int_0^{2L}k(x)\,\big(g(0)-g(x)\big)\,dx
        -c_L\,g(0)+\Pi(h),
\]
\[
  k(x)=\frac{e^{x/2}}{\sinh x},\qquad
  c_L=\int_0^{2L}\Big(k(x)-\frac1x\Big)dx+\log(4L)+\gamma+\log\pi,
\]
\[
  \Pi(h)=2\Big(\langle h,\cosh(x/2)\rangle^2
              -\langle h,\sinh(x/2)\rangle^2\Big).
\]
Equivalently, since $g(0)-g(x)=\tfrac12\|h-\tau_xh\|_2^2$,
\[
  \QW(h)=\tfrac12\int_0^{2L}k(x)\,\|h-\tau_xh\|_2^2\,dx
        +\Pi(h)-c_L\|h\|_2^2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\noindent\emph{Derivation.}
Gauss' integral
$\psi(z)=\int_0^\infty\big(e^{-v}/v-e^{-zv}/(1-e^{-v})\big)dv$
at $z=\tfrac14+it/2$, real part, then Plancherel with
$(2\pi)^{-1}\int\hat g(t)\cos(tv/2)\,dt=g(v/2)$ and
$(2\pi)^{-1}\int\hat g=g(0)$, gives
$A(h)=\int_0^\infty\big(g(0)e^{-2x}/x-g(x)k(x)\big)dx
-g(0)\log\pi$
after $v=2x$ and
$2e^{-x/2}/(1-e^{-2x})=e^{x/2}/\sinh x$.
Splitting at $2L$ (where $g$ vanishes) and using
$\int_\epsilon^{2L}dx/x-\int_\epsilon^\infty e^{-2x}dx/x
\to\log(4L)+\gamma$
produces $c_L$.
The pole term is
$\hat g(i/2)+\hat g(-i/2)=2\hat h(i/2)\hat h(-i/2)
=2(c^2-s^2)$ with $c=\langle h,\cosh(x/2)\rangle$,
$s=\langle h,\sinh(x/2)\rangle$.
At $L=0.3$: $\int_0^{0.6}(k-1/x)=0.288137803587$ and
$c_L=2.192404911132$.\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Why the tension dissolves}

\begin{lemma}[Loewner monotonicity of the $x$-truncation]
\label{lem:loewner}
For every $x$, $I-\operatorname{Re}\tau_x\succeq0$ on
$L^2[-L,L]$, since
$\langle h,(I-\operatorname{Re}\tau_x)h\rangle
=\tfrac12\|h-\tau_xh\|^2$.
Hence for every measurable $0\le w\le1$,
\[
  \QW\;\succeq\;\QW^{(w)}
  :=(\kappa_w-c_L)\,I-T_w+\Pi,\qquad
  \kappa_w=\int_0^{2L}w\,k ,
\]
where $T_w$ is the integral operator on $[-L,L]^2$ with kernel
$\tfrac12\,w(|y-y'|)\,k(|y-y'|)$.
\end{lemma}

This single line is the whole content of the verdict.
The structural tension named in r490 --- ``low-mode
certificates need living tails, completeness needs off-space
below the margin'' --- is a property of the frequency
coordinate.  In the $x$-coordinate:

\begin{itemize}
\item every truncation has a definite sign, so there is
\textbf{no residual and no off-space term} to bound, ever;
\item the truncated operator is \textbf{bounded and
Hilbert--Schmidt} once $w$ vanishes near $0$, with an
\textbf{explicit elementary kernel} on the compact box
$[-L,L]^2$;
\item there is \textbf{no infinite frequency range}, no
oscillatory integrand, no integration by parts, no endpoint
jet, no Slepian, no Nystr\"om nullspace;
\item choosing $w$ smooth makes the kernel $C^\infty$, so the
finite-section remainder decays fast and is itself an explicit
integral.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The honest hitches}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The quantitative hardness of r490 survives: the required

\section{Free diagnosis: $R$ \emph{is} coercive}\label{sec:coerc}

r488 concludes ``$R$ is positive semi-definite but not
coercive'' and ``the infimum of the form $R$ on $L^2[-L,L]$ is
$0$'', from $\lambda_{\min}(R)=0.337,0.106,0.076$ at
$n=48,96,128$.  That inference is false.
For $u\in L^2[-L,L]$ and any $t_1>t_c$, since $\sigma_A\ge0$
on $[t_c,\infty)$ and is increasing,
\[
  \langle u,A^+u\rangle
  \ \ge\ \sigma_A(t_1)\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\ge t_1}|\hat u|^2
  \ \ge\ \sigma_A(t_1)\big(1-\lambda_0(K_{t_1})\big)\|u\|^2 ,
\]
because $\lambda_0(K_{t_1})<1$ strictly for every finite $t_1$.
Hence $R\succeq A^+\succeq c_R I$ with
\[
  c_R=\sup_{t_1>t_c}\sigma_A(t_1)\big(1-\lambda_0(K_{t_1})\big)
  =1.5685\cdot10^{-2}\quad\text{at }t_1=8.2 .
\]
(The Slepian computation is calibrated on the sealed pins:
$\lambda_0(K_{t_c})=0.8593909212$ against $0.859391$,
$\lambda_0(K_8)=0.9350520466$ against $0.935$.)
Consequences:
the shift $s=0.05$ is not mandatory;
the observed $n$-decay of $\lambda_{\min}(R)$ is convergence to
something $\ge1.57\cdot10^{-2}$, not to $0$.
This does \emph{not} rescue the lane --- the residual amplifier
becomes $1/c_R=64$ instead of $1/s=20$, i.e.\ worse --- but the
false premise must not be carried forward as a pin.
It is added to the anti-list as a false premise, and it is the
reason the unshifted form of (b)(ii) is at least well posed.

\section{U2 --- is the keystone worth the price?}

Yes, but only in the new form and only under a budget.
Three facts decide it.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The price collapsed.}  In the frequency form the
remainder was an ideas problem with no proposal left after ten
rounds.  In the kernel form the remainder is one bounded
eigenvalue bound with a measured closing budget.  One to three
rounds, not ten.
\item \textbf{It dominates the standing fallback.}  r471
certified $Q_L\succeq0$ on the grid family up to
$L_{16}=2.7726$; its named gap F2b is fixed-$L$ density plus a
continuity modulus, made necessary because point evaluations
are not $L^2$-continuous.  The kernel lane never evaluates $g$
at a point: it produces $\lambda_*(L)\ge c$ on all of
$L^2[-L,L]$ directly, so wherever it executes it makes F2b
redundant.  F2b is demoted to fallback.
\item \textbf{It scales better than the frequency route.}
For $2L\ge\log2$ the prime term enters the same $x$-space form
as an explicit \emph{bounded} perturbation
$-\sum_{n\le e^{2L}}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\,(\tau_{\log n}
+\tau_{-\log n})$
(three terms at $L=0.8$: $n=2,3,4$), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:loewner} is unaffected.
Compare the frequency route, where $\sigma_P$ is almost
periodic and the negative set of $\sigma_A-\sigma_P$ is
multi-part.
\end{enumerate}
