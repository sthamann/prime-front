\title{\bfseries The length invariant $X_n$ of a folded sign field\\[2mm]
\large How pairing reduces MED-CAP to an edge-return,
and the precise obstruction that remains}
\author{Stefan Hamann}
\date{August 28, 2026}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}\noindent
The median-cap inequality $\mathrm{med}_i\le\tfrac83\,\mathrm{sep}_i$
on a tiled block packing is equivalent to a constraint $X_n$ on the
length sequence $(n_i)$.  Interval geometry does not prove $X_n$: the
tiled composition $n=(1,2,8^{10})$ violates it.  This note proves the
combinatorial half.  Blocks are paired consecutive same-sign runs of a
$\pm$ colouring of a tiled integer interval, with an odd tail its own
block, pairing from $x$-min.  Then $n=1$ occurs only as that odd tail;
the tail sits at $\theta$-min; the prefixes $(2,1)$ and $(1,1)$ are
impossible; and the unique possible locus with $\mathrm{sep}_i=\tfrac32$
is the edge pair $(n_0,n_1)=(1,2)$.  Interior small-separation does not
exist.  Pairing does \emph{not} forbid $C_2$: random paired run sequences
still violate MED-CAP in a few percent of draws.  What pairing does is
isolate the only pattern that can violate --- a length-$1$ tail, two
further singleton runs, and a subsequent plateau of long runs.
On the source construction that pattern occurs once among $134$ windows
($15\,428$ atoms): the saturating prefix $(1,2,6,5,4,4)$ of $\chi_4$ at
index $53$, with the nearest $n\ge 7$ at index $7$ (outside the
radius-$5$ window).  The named remainder is a local spacing statement
for the bordered-chain polynomial $v_2$ at the $20\%$ $x$-mask: after
three consecutive singleton runs the next four runs must not form a
plateau of length $\ge 3$.  The minimal grid-consistent violator is the
run sequence ending $(\ldots,{\ge}3,{\ge}3,1,1,1)$.  It is not realised
on the surface.  Nothing in this document is evidence for or against
the Riemann hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Recollection}\label{sec:rec}

We use the packing notation of~\cite{medcap} without change.  Blocks
$I_i\subset\Z$ of lengths $n_i$ are pairwise disjoint and ordered;
$c_i$ is the unweighted centre; $\mathrm{sep}_i$ is the cut-geometry
lower bound $\min(n_i+n_j)/2$ on adjacent sides;
$\mathrm{med}_i$ is the radius-$W=5$ window median of neighbouring
gaps.  MED-CAP is $\mathrm{med}_i\le\tfrac83\,\mathrm{sep}_i$ for every
$i$.  On a \emph{tiled} packing (no unoccupied integer between or
inside blocks) one has $\mathrm{gap}_i=\mathrm{sep}_i$ identically, and
MED-CAP becomes a statement about $(n_i)$ alone.  That tiling identity
holds on $134$ windows / $15\,428$ atoms of the source~\cite{medcap}.
The obstruction $X_n$ is that $(n_i)$ must not realise, at a
small-separation locus
($\mathrm{sep}_i=\tfrac32$ from an adjacent $(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$, or
$\mathrm{sep}_i=1$ from two adjacent singletons), a radius-$5$ window
whose third-smallest neighbour-gap (edge) or mean of the $5$th and
$6$th (interior) exceeds $\tfrac83\,\mathrm{sep}_i$.

The blocks of the source are not an abstract composition.  They are
read off a $\pm$ colouring of the tiled $\theta$-grid, as follows.

\section{The sign field}\label{sec:sign}

A window of depth $N$ carries two discrete measures on $[-1,1]$: the
arithmetic comb (prime-power, Dirichlet, or second-family) and a
smooth border comb.  Each is obtained by the fold
\[
j \;\longmapsto\; \min(j,\,L-j),\qquad
x=\cos\bigl(2\pi\,\min(j,L-j)/L\bigr),
\]
applied separately to the positive and negative cells of a lag-density
$d_{\mathrm{arm}}$ on a circular grid of $L=2M-2$ points
($\mu$~keeps $d_{\mathrm{arm}}>0$, $\nu$~keeps $d_{\mathrm{arm}}<0$).
Fold multiplicity is at most $2$ (the two preimages $j$ and $L-j$).
The mesh identity of the frame ($h-\mathrm{NU}\,u\in(0,\tfrac32]$)
makes the $\theta$-grid
$t=\arccos(x)\cdot(4N-2)/(2\pi)$ integral on every surviving atom.

Level-$2$ blocks are defined on the \emph{smooth border} bulk, after a
$20\%$ $x$-hull edge mask.  Let $c_t$ be the bulk contributions
$c_t=w\,x\,v_2\,\phi$, where $w$ is the folded smooth weight
($w>0$ on $\mu$, $w<0$ on $\nu$), $x$ is the fold coordinate, $v_2$ is
the degree-$(N-2)$ bordered-chain polynomial evaluated on the border
nodes, and $\phi$ is a global scalar.  Maximal same-sign runs of $c_t$
in increasing-$x$ order are paired from offset $0$; an odd tail is its
own block.  Window (arithmetic) atoms are then assigned to those
blocks by $x$-breakpoint search.  The length $n_i$ is the number of
distinct $\theta$-grid integers in block $i$.

\begin{lemma}[sign identity and occupation]\label{lem:n-from-sign}
On the four $w=9$ worlds and on $\chi_4$ at index $53$,
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathrm{sign}(c_t)=\mathrm{sign}(w)\,\mathrm{sign}(x)\,\mathrm{sign}(v_2)$
at every bulk atom ($0$ disagreements among $150+74+150+150+496$
atoms);
\item $n_i$ equals the number of distinct $\theta$-grid integers occupied
by \emph{smooth-border} atoms of the block --- arithmetic window atoms
add zero cells;
\item $n_i$ equals the sum of the paired run lengths of that colouring,
measured in distinct grid integers.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the length sequence is a function of the $\pm$ colouring
of the tiled $\theta$-grid of the smooth border, and of nothing else.
\end{lemma}

The identity in (i) is the product rule for $c_t=w\,x\,v_2\,\phi$.
Parts (ii)--(iii) are the fold-group ledger against the pairing
definition; they are re-gated by exact integer counting in the
companion script.  On the $w=9$ worlds the smooth weight $w$ has a
single sign-run on the whole bulk, so the colouring is
$\mathrm{sign}(v_2)$ times a global sign times $\mathrm{sign}(x)$
(one flip at $x=0$).  At $\chi_4$ index $53$ the weight $w$ has
fourteen sign-runs; $v_2$ still dominates (run-length mode $2$).

\section{What pairing proves}\label{sec:pair}

Fix a finite sequence of positive integers $(r_0,\dots,r_{\nu-1})$,
the grid lengths of consecutive same-sign runs in increasing-$x$
order.  Pairing from offset $0$ produces the $x$-order lengths
\[
n^{\mathrm{x}}_{j}
 \;=\;
 r_{2j}+r_{2j+1}
 \quad(2j+1<\nu),\qquad
 n^{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{last}}=r_{\nu-1}
 \text{ if $\nu$ is odd}.
\]
Because $x=\cos\theta$ is decreasing in $\theta$ on $[0,\pi]$,
$x$-max is $\theta$-min.  The odd tail is therefore block $0$ in
$\theta$-order, and the $\theta$-order sequence is the reverse of the
$x$-order sequence.

\begin{lemma}[$n=1$ only as odd tail]\label{lem:n1-tail}
If every $r_k\ge 1$, then $n_i=1$ occurs if and only if $\nu$ is odd,
$r_{\nu-1}=1$, and $i=0$ in $\theta$-order.  In particular $n=1$ cannot
occur in the interior, and cannot occur as a paired block.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A paired block is a sum of two positive integers, hence at least $2$.
The only remaining possibility is the odd tail, of length $r_{\nu-1}$.
That tail is $\theta$-index $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[forbidden prefixes]\label{lem:no-21}
The $\theta$-order prefixes $(n_0,n_1)=(2,1)$ and $(1,1)$ cannot occur.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(1,1)$ would require a tail of length $1$ and a previous pair summing
to $1$.  Two positive integers cannot sum to $1$.  $(2,1)$ would
require a tail of length $1$ at $\theta$-index $1$, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{lem:n1-tail}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[unique small-separation locus]\label{lem:unique-12}
On any tiled packing obtained by pairing a positive run sequence,
$\mathrm{sep}_i\le\tfrac32$ occurs if and only if
$(n_0,n_1)=(1,2)$, and then only at $i=0$ and $i=1$, with
$\mathrm{sep}_0=\mathrm{sep}_1=\tfrac32$.  In particular
$\mathrm{sep}_i=1$ (two adjacent singletons) is impossible.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{sep}_i=\tfrac12(n_i+n_j)$ for an adjacent pair, so
$\mathrm{sep}\le\tfrac32$ forces $\{n_i,n_j\}\in\{\{1,1\},\{1,2\}\}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:n1-tail} puts every $1$ at index $0$;
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-21} excludes $(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$.  The remaining
possibility is $(n_0,n_1)=(1,2)$.
\end{proof}

Thus $X_n$ --- which constrains the radius-$5$ window at small-separation
loci --- is a statement about a single edge, when that edge exists, and
is vacuous on every packing whose odd tail is longer than $1$ or whose
run count is even.

\begin{lemma}[pairing does not forbid $C_2$]\label{lem:c2-lives}
The run sequence whose $\theta$-order lengths are
$n=(1,2,8^{10})$ is a legal pairing of positive run lengths
(tail $1$, previous pair $1+1$, then pairs summing to $8$).  The
tiled packing of that sequence violates MED-CAP at $i=0,1$ with
ratio $16/3$.  Among $1259$ random paired sequences of run lengths
in $\{1,\ldots,8\}$, a positive fraction (here $32$) violate
MED-CAP.  A Chebyshev colouring $\mathrm{sign}(\sin m\theta)$ on a
uniform $\theta$-grid, after the same $20\%$ $x$-mask, produces $0$
violations among $123$ windows and $0$ prefixes $(1,2)$.
\end{lemma}

The Chebyshev comparison is the reason the bulk mode is $n=4$: zeros
uniform in $\theta$ with spacing $\pi/N$ against a grid of spacing
$\pi/(2N)$ give run length $2$, and pairing two such runs gives $4$.
An $x$-mask of a Chebyshev colouring cuts one run into a stub and
leaves prefix $(1,4,4,\ldots)$ typically, never $(1,2)$.  The
prefix $(1,2)$ is a \emph{spacing gradient} at the mask --- three
consecutive singleton runs --- which a uniform-in-$\theta$ colouring
does not produce.

\section{What $X_n$ says at the only possible locus}\label{sec:return}

\begin{lemma}[order-statistic content of $X_n$]\label{lem:return-geom}
Let $n=(1,2,n_2,\ldots)$ be tiled, $m\ge 6$.  Write $g_i$ for the
gaps (equal to sides).  Then $g_0=g_1=\tfrac32$, and the five
neighbour-gaps of $i=0$ are
$(g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4,g_5)$.  MED-CAP at $i=0$ is equivalent to the
third-smallest of those five being $\le 4$.  In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item the saturating prefix $(1,2,6,5,4,4)$ has third-smallest $4$
and ratio $8/3$;
\item a single spike $(1,2,7,4,4,4)$ likewise has third-smallest $4$
(an isolated $n=7$ with immediate return to the bulk mode does
\emph{not} violate);
\item the short $C_2$ prefix $(1,2,6,6,6,6)$ has third-smallest $6$
and ratio $4$;
\item the long $C_2$ prefix $(1,2,8^{10})$ has third-smallest $8$
and ratio $16/3$.
\end{itemize}
Hence $X_n$ at its only possible locus forbids a \emph{plateau} of
long runs after the $(1,2)$, not a single overshoot.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation of sides, gaps, and the third-of-five, as in
\cite{medcap} Lemmas~3--4, plus the spike $(1,2,7,4,4,4)$:
sides $\tfrac32,\,\tfrac92,\,\tfrac{11}2,\,4,\,4$; neighbour-gaps of
$i=0$ sort as $(\tfrac32,\,4,\,4,\,4,\,\tfrac92)$; the third is $4$.
\end{proof}

The medcap formulation ``$n\ge 7$ at that locus is forbidden'' is
therefore slightly too strong as a \emph{sufficient} picture: a
lone $7$ with return to $4$ saturates.  It is the right picture
\emph{in substance}, because a $7$ that does not return produces
three neighbour-gaps $>4$ and violates.  The precise statement is
Lemma~\ref{lem:return-geom}.

\section{The source surface}\label{sec:census}

\begin{prop}[one-shot census]\label{prop:surface}
On the $134$ windows of \cite{medcap} Proposition~5
($42$-rung prime-power core $+\,8$ second-family anchors $+\,42+42$
Dirichlet matchings; $15\,428$ blocks; tiling exact, $0$ empty
sides, $0$ hull holes, $0$ MED-CAP violations):
\begin{enumerate}
\item $n=1$ occurs $32$ times, always at $\theta$-index $0$, always
as an odd tail of length $1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:n1-tail} with no
exceptions);
\item of those $32$, the next length is $n_1=4$ in $24$ windows
(the Chebyshev stub), $n_1=5$ in four, $n_1=8$ in two, $n_1=3$ in
one, and $n_1=2$ in \emph{one};
\item that one $(1,2)$ is $\chi_4$ at index $53$, prefix
$(1,2,6,5,4,4,4,8)$, third-smallest neighbour-gap $4$, ratio
$8/3$, nearest $n\ge 7$ at index $7$ (distance $7$ from $i=0$,
outside the radius-$5$ window);
\item consequently there are exactly two small-separation atoms on
the whole surface (indices $0$ and $1$ of that window), and $X_n$
holds at both, saturating.
\end{enumerate}
The block-length histogram is $n=4$ on $9006$ atoms ($58.4\%$),
with a right tail out to $n=16$ (once).  Long blocks exist, in the
interior.  They are never adjacent to a $(1,2)$.
\end{prop}

The $114$ atoms of \cite{medcap} Proposition~6 --- blocks with
$n_i\le 2$ that see some $n_j\ge 7$ inside the radius-$5$ window ---
are almost all interior $n=2$ against a generous separation
$\mathrm{sep}\ge\tfrac52$.  They are not small-separation loci, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-12} says they cannot be.  The worst such ratio
$12/5$ is at $\chi_3$ index $20$, prefix $(1,4,8,4,8,4)$,
$\mathrm{sep}=\tfrac52$.

\section{The remainder}\label{sec:remainder}

Lemmas~\ref{lem:n1-tail}--\ref{lem:unique-12} reduce $X_n$ to a
single edge-return.  Proposition~\ref{prop:surface} verifies the
return on every window the construction currently produces.  What
is \emph{not} proved as a cofinal law is that a deeper (or
hypothetical) window cannot realise the plateau.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Minimal grid-consistent violator.}\enspace
The run sequence in $x$-order ending
\[
(\ldots,\; r,\; r,\; 1,\; 1,\; 1)
\qquad\text{with }r\ge 3
\]
pairs to $\theta$-order $n=(1,2,2r,\ldots)$.  Already $r=3$ gives
the short $C_2$ prefix $(1,2,6,6,\ldots)$ once the plateau continues
for four runs; $r=4$ gives $(1,2,8,8,\ldots)$.  This colouring is
legal as a $\pm$ assignment of a tiled interval, legal as a pairing,
and legal as the restriction of a degree-$(N-2)$ polynomial (a
colouring with $k$ runs needs $k-1$ zeros).  Fold multiplicity
$\le 2$ and the mesh identity do not forbid it: they constrain how
many source atoms land on a cell, not the run lengths of $v_2$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Named condition $V_2$.}\enspace
Let $v_2$ be the degree-$(N-2)$ bordered-chain polynomial of the
window.  Write $R_{\mathrm{mask}}$ for the run lengths of
$\mathrm{sign}(v_2\,w\,x)$ on the bulk $\theta$-grid, in $x$-order.
Condition $V_2$ is: if the last three values of $R_{\mathrm{mask}}$
are $(1,1,1)$, then among the four preceding run lengths at least
two are $\le 2$ (no $6$-plateau at the mask).  Equivalently, $v_2$
has no local $3\times$ dilation of zero spacing immediately after a
triple sign-flip at the $20\%$ $x$-mask.

$V_2$ is the OP-spacing analogue (Freud / arcsine equilibrium
spacing of consecutive zeros) for this specific polynomial.  It is
\emph{not} a cited classical theorem: $v_2$ is a bordered-chain
polynomial, not the orthogonal polynomial of a fixed positive
continuous measure.  A Chebyshev colouring satisfies $V_2$ vacuously
(it never produces three consecutive $1$-runs after an $x$-mask).
The source $v_2$ satisfies $V_2$ on all $134$ windows.  The bulk
mode $n=4$ is the global counterpart of the same spacing (average
run length $2$, pairing to $4$).

\begin{prop}[conditional MED-CAP]\label{prop:conditional}
On every tiled packing produced by the construction of
\S\ref{sec:sign},
\[
X_{\mathrm{tile}}
 \;+\;
\text{Lemmas~\ref{lem:n1-tail}--\ref{lem:unique-12}}
 \;+\;
 V_2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
 X_n
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{MED-CAP}.
\]
Hence, on that packing, $g_i=\mathrm{gap}_i/\mathrm{med}_i\ge 3/8$
pointwise, $S_r=\sum_{g_i\le 2^{-r}}q_i=0$ for every $r\ge 2$, and
$T_2'$ is trivial.  The cubic-mass bound
$M_3\le(\tfrac83)^2 C_K^2(\log m)^2/m^2$ of \cite{medcap}~\S T1
carries, still carrying $C_K$.
\end{prop}

On the $134$-window surface $V_2$ is verified, so the implications
hold unconditionally \emph{there}.  A cofinal theorem would need
$V_2$ as a lemma about the bordered chain, or a replacement that
bounds consecutive $v_2$-zero spacings at the mask.  Degree counting
alone does not give $V_2$: a degree-$(N-2)$ polynomial may cluster
its zeros and leave a gap.

\section{T1}\label{sec:t1}

The conclusion of \cite{medcap}~\S T1 stands.  $X_n$ is a constraint
on run lengths of a sign field.  The masses $q_i$ see the same field
as an amplitude.  Proving $X_n$, even cofinally, does not produce a
family-uniform $C_K$.  The r358 record (per-family freeze maximum
fails on the test set; ceiling $23.70$ against freeze $4.91$) is
unchanged.  After MED-CAP, T1 remains the single quantitative rest
of the terminal lane.

\section*{Claim boundary}

Finite identities on an explicit family of packings, a pairing
theorem for positive run sequences, and a named analytic remainder
$V_2$ on a bordered-chain polynomial.  No statement about zeros of
$\zeta(s)$, in either direction.  No RH claim.
