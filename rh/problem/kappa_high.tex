\begin{abstract}\noindent
Round 479 reduced $\lambda_*(0.3)\ge c$ to a single operator-norm
bound: $\kappa_{\mathrm{high}}=\|P_5A_+(1-P_5)\|\le1.79\cdot10^{-2}$
at the split $(t_*,N)=(8,5)$, with
$A_+=\sigma_A\,1_{|t|\ge8}$ and $P_5$ the projection on the first
five Slepian modes of band~$8$ on $L^2[-0.3,0.3]$.
Nystr\"om-stable quadrature kills that inequality:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{high}}\approx2.91\cdot10^{-1}$
(sixteen times the budget), and every scanned rebalance of
$(t_*,N)$ stays in $[0.29,0.40]$.
The crude test compares the edge-mode singular value
$\psi_4\leftrightarrow\psi_6$ to the ground-mode eigenvalue ---
structurally the wrong pairing.
The even $3\times3$ matrix Schur
$S=B-CC^\top/c_{\mathrm{tail}}$ is numerically positive
($\lambda_{\min}(S)\approx5.69\cdot10^{-3}$ at $n=48$, $T=150$;
stable versus $n=64$).
Proving $S\succ0$ with an interval Fourier transform on $[8,T]$
and a $1/t^2$ integration-by-parts tail would close
$\lambda_*(0.3)\ge c$.
That enclosure is open.
Verdict \texttt{REDUCED(refined-Schur; crude-$\kappa_{\mathrm{high}}$-killed@$0.30$)}.
The Fourier--HS majorant of r478 is not reused.
$L=0.3$ is inside the Yoshida--Bombieri zone.
No RH claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{The named bound, and why it fails}

Write $K_8=P_LB_8P_L$ and let $\{\psi_n\}$ be its Slepian basis
on $L^2[-0.3,0.3]$.  Then $K_8$ is diagonal,
so the low-band piece of $P_5A(1-P_5)$ obeys
$\kappa_{\mathrm{low}}\le|\sigma_A(0)|\sqrt{\varepsilon}$
with $\varepsilon=\lambda_6(K_8)\le1.4335\cdot10^{-6}$
(Nystr\"om-stable, r479).
The high-band piece is
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{high}}
 =\bigl\|P_5A_+(1-P_5)\bigr\|,
 \qquad
 A_+=\sigma_A\,1_{|t|\ge8}.
\]
In matrix form this is the $2$-norm of the off-block
$C_{mn}=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{|t|\ge8}\sigma_A(t)\,\widehat\psi_m(t)
\overline{\widehat\psi_n(t)}\,dt$
for $m<5\le n$.

\begin{theorem}[Kill of the operator-norm budget]\label{thm:kill}
At $(t_*,N)=(8,5)$, Nystr\"om $n=48$ with trapezoid quadrature
on $[8,150]$ gives
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{high}}\approx2.905\cdot10^{-1}
 \;\not\le\;
 1.79\cdot10^{-2}.
\]
The same floor $\kappa_{\mathrm{high}}>0.25$ holds at
$(8,6)$, $(10,6)$ and $(12,7)$.
$n=64$ moves the value by less than $5\cdot10^{-3}$.
Even--odd entries of $A_+$ vanish
($A_+[4,5]\sim10^{-12}$).
The large entries are same-parity edge couplings
$A_+[3,5]\approx0.213$, $A_+[4,6]\approx0.162$.
\end{theorem}

Modes $n=3,4$ of band~$8$ have high-band mass $1-\lambda_n=O(1)$.
The tail $n\ge5$ is equally high-frequency.
Cauchy--Schwarz on
$\langle\psi_m,\sigma_A1_{\ge8}v\rangle$
with both sides of mass $\Theta(1)$ produces an $O(\sigma_A)$
coupling, in the same range as the measured singular value.
Raising $t_*$ or $N$ moves the edge but does not remove it:
the time-bandwidth transition always sits between the block
and the tail.

The r479 budget
$\sqrt{\mu_3\,c_{\mathrm{tail}}}-\kappa_{\mathrm{low}}\le1.79\cdot10^{-2}$
compares that edge singular value to the even Fourier
Higham floor $\mu_3\ge2.457\cdot10^{-3}$.
Those two numbers live on different directions
(the ground prolate versus $\psi_4\leftrightarrow\psi_6$).
The comparison is not forced, and the inequality is false.

The r478 Fourier--HS majorant ($\mathrm{HS}\sim0.27$) is a
different operator and is not reused.

\section{Slepian structure and the $1/t$ tail}

On the low band, $1_{|t|<8}=B_8$ and $P_LB_8P_L=K_8$ is
diagonal, so $P_5\leftrightarrow(1-P_5)$ through $K_8$ is
exactly zero.  All coupling of $A_+$ is weighted high-band
overlap.
The Fourier transform of a time-limited prolate does
\emph{not} decay super-exponentially:
$\psi_n(\pm L)\ne0$, and one integration by parts gives
\[
 \bigl|\widehat\psi_n(t)\bigr|
 \;\le\;
 \frac{|\psi_n(L)|+|\psi_n(-L)|}{|t|}
 +\frac{\|\psi_n'\|_1}{|t|}.
\]
A second integration produces a $1/t^2$ majorant with
explicit endpoint and $\|\psi_n''\|_1$ terms.
Then $\int_T^\infty(\log t)/t^2\,dt=(1+\log T)/T<\infty$,
so a high-frequency remainder is possible in principle.
At $T=150$ the elementary integral equals $4.01\cdot10^{-2}$.
Endpoint values of $\psi_0$ are Nystr\"om-stable
($n=48$ versus $n=64$ differ by $<0.02$).
$n=16$ --- sufficient for the r479 eigenvalues ---
is unusable for Fourier transforms:
the constant-mode Rayleigh $Q(\psi_0)$ jumps by more than $1$.
Quadrature of $\widehat\psi_n(t)=\sum_i w_i\psi_n(x_i)e^{itx_i}$
dies once $t$ outruns the Gauss nodes
($T=400$ at $n=48$ is garbage; the working window is $T\le150$).

\section{The refined remainder}

Let $B$ be the even $3\times3$ Gram of $\QW$ on
$\mathrm{span}\{\psi_0,\psi_2,\psi_4\}$ and $C$ the coupling
of that block to the even tail $\{\psi_6,\psi_8,\dots\}$.
On the $K$-tail, r479 gives $A\ge c_{\mathrm{tail}}\ge0.2408$.
The block-matrix
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}B&C\\C^\top&T\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
is positive definite once $B\succ0$, $T\succeq c_{\mathrm{tail}}I$,
and
\[
 S \;=\; B-\frac{CC^\top}{c_{\mathrm{tail}}} \;\succ\;0.
\]
The large entries of $C$ sit in the $\psi_4$ row, where
$B_{44}\approx1.3$; the ground row of $C$ is $O(10^{-2})$.

\begin{theorem}[Numeric matrix Schur]\label{thm:schur}
At $(8,5)$, $n=48$, $T=150$:
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(B)\approx6.66\cdot10^{-3},\qquad
 \|C\|\approx0.206,\qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(S)\approx5.69\cdot10^{-3}.
\]
The odd sector has $\lambda_{\min}(S)\approx0.207$.
$n=64$ changes $\lambda_{\min}(S)$ by less than $5\cdot10^{-4}$.
These are working constants, not a Lipschitz-interval enclosure
of either the prolates or the Fourier integrals.
\end{theorem}

If $S\succ0$ is proved with a quadrature remainder on $[8,T]$
and the integration-by-parts tail on $[T,\infty)$, then
$\lambda_*(0.3)\ge c$ for an explicit $c>0$
(odd sector strictly easier; $\Pi$ is rank one per parity).
That is the exact remainder after this round.
