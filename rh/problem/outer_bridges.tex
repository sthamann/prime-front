\section{Dense bridge reduction}

\code{FullWeilTest} now records a continuous even function supported in
\([ -R,R]\), \(R\geq0\), together with an explicit real
autocorrelation witness
\[
 g(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}h(t)h(t+u)\,dt .
\]
\code{FixedSupportGridApproximation} requires support preservation,
pointwise convergence on the support, and \(L^1([-R,R])\) convergence.
The open construction is exactly
\code{fullWeil_fixedSupport_grid_density}.

The form is decomposed visibly into archimedean, von-Mangoldt and polar
channels.  Their continuity is the second named brick
\code{fullWeil_channel_continuity}.  Once these two bricks are supplied,
the following are proved in Lean:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{fullWeilForm_tendsto_of_channels};
\item \code{fullWeilForm_nonneg_of_tendsto};
\item \code{grid_dense_extension_of_fixedSupport};
\item the public wrapper \code{grid_dense_extension}.
\end{itemize}
The positivity step uses closedness of \([0,\infty)\), through
\code{ge_of_tendsto}; no mesh-PSD transport or infinite-window research
hypothesis is introduced.

\section{Zeta-interface design}

Mathlib 4.29.1 defines
\[
\text{\ttfamily RiemannHypothesis}
=\{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ \rho\hbox{ nontrivial},\ \rho\ne1
\Longrightarrow \Re\rho=\tfrac12\}.
\]
There is no \code{ZetaZero} structure or enumeration.  R487 therefore
uses a direct predicate built from \code{riemannZeta s = 0}.

The spectral target is the named proposition
\code{GuinandWeilExplicitFormula}: the arithmetic three-channel form
equals the multiplicity-weighted zero spectral form, once that spectral
API has been built.  The criterion target is
\code{FullWeilSeparatesOffCriticalZeros}: each off-critical nontrivial
zero admits an admissible autocorrelation with negative standard form.
The theorem
\code{standard_weil_criterion_to_mathlib_rh_of_separation} proves the
remaining logical implication by contradiction, and the public
\code{standard_weil_criterion_to_mathlib_rh} is its wrapper.

References for the future theorem are Weil, \emph{Sur les formules
explicites de la théorie des nombres premiers} (1952), and Bombieri,
\emph{The Riemann Hypothesis} (Clay Mathematics Institute, 2000).
