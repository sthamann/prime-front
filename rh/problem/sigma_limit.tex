\section{The lemma}\label{sec:lem}

\begin{lemma}[$\Sigma$ limit]\label{lem:sig}
On the selected sequence $a_k=2^k$,
$\Sigma_k=2s^{T}(C+I)(C-I)^{-1}s$ stays bounded
with
$\limsup_k\Sigma_k<|c_{J,\infty}|=2-\mathrm{den}_\infty$,
source-pure.
\end{lemma}

\noindent
\textbf{Verdict: REDUZIERT.}
$\Sigma=2\|s\|^{2}+4m_{\mathrm{occ}}(1)$ is exact.
Near-$1$ mass is stable $O(0.01)$: the
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ growth is bulk broadening, not
pole-creep.
EXT large-$N$ values of $\Sigma$ stay below $0.43$,
not above the selected $k=9$ value $0.480$.
But $\mathrm{den}_\infty$ and $\Sigma_\infty$ are
not constructed ($\mathrm{den}$ still wanders in
$[1.48,1.65]$).
The inequality remains a census.

\section{The Stieltjes identity}\label{sec:st}

Write $g(\lambda)=(\lambda+1)/(\lambda-1)$ and
$\mathrm{occ}_i=|\langle\psi_i,s\rangle|^{2}$ for
the eigenpairs of $C$.
Then $g(\lambda)=1+2/(\lambda-1)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:st}
\Sigma
\;=\;
2\sum_i\mathrm{occ}_i\,g(\lambda_i)
\;=\;
2\|s\|^{2}+4\,s^{T}(C-I)^{-1}s
\;=\;
2\|s\|^{2}+4\,m_{\mathrm{occ}}(1).
\end{equation}
Over $\mathbb{Q}$, $C=\mathrm{diag}(2,4)$,
$s=(1,1)$ gives $\Sigma=\tfrac{28}{3}$.
On the construction the residual against
$s^{T}A_{0}^{-1}s$ is $10^{-13}$--$10^{-9}$.

\begin{prop}[Stieltjes form]\label{prop:st}
\eqref{eq:st} holds as a finite identity
(r407: $A_{0}^{-1}=2(C+I)(C-I)^{-1}$).
Dropping the pole ($g\equiv 1$) yields
$2\|s\|^{2}$, which is not $\Sigma$
($w9$: $0.111$ versus $0.313$).
\end{prop}

This \emph{is} the source-pure form: $m_{\mathrm{occ}}(1)$
is the Stieltjes transform of the occupancy measure
at the pole.
It is not yet an elementary source sum.

\section{No closed occupancy density}\label{sec:dens}

\begin{prop}[closed density]\label{prop:dens}
A one-pole ansatz $m(1)\approx\|s\|^{2}/(\lambda_w-1)$
misses $m(1)$ by relative error $0.73$ on every
selected window.
A two-moment Cauchy ansatz misses by $0.95$.
No elementary $\mathrm{occ}(\lambda)$
(in particular, not the derivative of an
explicit scalar Stieltjes transform built from
$\lambda_w$ or the first two moments) is obtained.
\end{prop}

The border direction $s$ is a CD-border column.
Its overlap with the $C$-modes is a genuine
spectral measure, but the r407 dictionaries do
not reduce it to a closed density in this round.

\section{The three numbers versus the floor}\label{sec:bal}

Pointwise $R=2-\mathrm{den}-\Sigma=-\phibb$ is the
r418/r420 identity.
On selected $k=5,6,7,9$ plus the $k=8$ pin,
$\mathrm{den}\in[1.48,1.65]$ and
$\Sigma\in[0.24,0.48]$.
EXT vacuous $\Sigma\in[0.405,0.430]$ at
$N=2472,3022$ --- not above $k=9$.
The floor $R_\infty\approx 0.030$ is the residual
of two $O(0.5)$ wanderers.
It is consistent with every finite row and is
\emph{not} a closed triple
$(\mathrm{den}_\infty,\Sigma_\infty,R_\infty)$.

If both limits existed with
$\Sigma_\infty<2-\mathrm{den}_\infty$, the cofinal
edge would be that inequality plus the finite
census $R>0$ on the named living windows.
Existence of the limits remains the missing
piece.

\section{What is a theorem, what is a census}\label{sec:honest}

Proposition~\ref{prop:st} is a theorem.
Proposition~\ref{prop:dens} is a named refutation.
Near-$1$ stability and the falling KS distances
are a census.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sig} is not a theorem:
boundedness of $\Sigma$ is observed, the pole is
not eating the mass, but
$\mathrm{den}_\infty$ and $\Sigma_\infty$ are not
built, so $\limsup\Sigma<2-\mathrm{den}_\infty$
stays conditional.
No RH claim.
