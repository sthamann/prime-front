\title{\bfseries A median cap for gaps of a tiled integer packing\\[2mm]
\large The inequality $\mathrm{med}_i\le\tfrac83\,\mathrm{sep}_i$,
proved as far as packing goes, and the precise obstruction beyond}
\author{Stefan Hamann}
\date{August 28, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}\noindent
On a finite string of contiguous, pairwise disjoint blocks of
positive integers, write $n_i$ for the length of block~$i$,
$c_i$ for its centre, $\mathrm{gap}_i$ for the one-sided nearest-centre
distance, and $\mathrm{sep}_i$ for the cut-geometry lower bound
$\min_j(n_i+n_j)/2$ on the two adjacent sides.  Normalise by the
radius-$5$ window median of neighbouring gaps.  The inequality
$\mathrm{med}_i\le\tfrac83\,\mathrm{sep}_i$ holds on every atom of a
specific tiled packing coming from a folded discrete measure
($0$ violations among $32\,728$ atoms, the constant saturated).
It is \emph{not} a theorem of interval geometry: already the
tiled composition $n=(1,2,8^{10})$ violates it, with ratio
$16/3$.  What packing \emph{does} prove is a clean reduction:
if the blocks tile an interval (no unoccupied integers between
or inside blocks), then $\mathrm{gap}_i=\mathrm{sep}_i$ identically
and the inequality becomes a statement about the length sequence
$(n_i)$ alone.  That tiling identity holds with $0$ exceptions
on $134$ windows ($15\,428$ atoms) of the source construction.
The remaining obstruction is named: the length sequence must
forbid a $C_2$ jump (a $(1,2)$-pair of short blocks looking, inside
the radius-$5$ window, at three or more large neighbour-gaps).
Sign-run pairing of an arbitrary $\pm$ colouring of the tiled
interval does \emph{not} forbid $C_2$.  This note is a proof
attempt with a named remainder, not a claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{The packing}\label{sec:pack}

Let $m\ge 2$.  A \emph{block packing} on $\Z$ is a sequence of
nonempty finite intervals
\[
I_i \;=\; \{L_i,\,L_i+1,\,\dots,\,L_i+n_i-1\}
\subset\Z,
\qquad n_i\in\N_{\ge 1},\quad i=0,\dots,m-1,
\]
pairwise disjoint, ordered so that $L_0<L_1<\cdots<L_{m-1}$.
Write $R_i=L_i+n_i-1$ for the right endpoint.  The
\emph{unoccupied stretch} between adjacent blocks is the nonnegative
integer
\[
e_i \;=\; L_{i+1}-R_i-1 \,\in\,\N\cup\{0\}.
\]
The packing is \emph{tiled} when $e_i=0$ for every $i$ (the union
$\bigcup I_i$ is itself an interval).  The \emph{centre} of block
$i$ is the unweighted mean of its integers,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centre}
c_i \;=\; L_i+\frac{n_i-1}{2} \;\in\; \tfrac12\Z.
\end{equation}
The adjacent \emph{sides} and the one-sided \emph{gaps} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sides}
d_i \;=\; c_{i+1}-c_i,
\qquad
\mathrm{gap}_i \;=\;
\begin{cases}
d_0, & i=0,\\
\min(d_{i-1},d_i), & 1\le i\le m-2,\\
d_{m-2}, & i=m-1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The \emph{separation} of an adjacent pair and the per-block
separation are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sep}
s_i \;=\; \frac{n_i+n_{i+1}}{2},
\qquad
\mathrm{sep}_i \;=\;
\begin{cases}
s_0, & i=0,\\
\min(s_{i-1},s_i), & 1\le i\le m-2,\\
s_{m-2}, & i=m-1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The radius-$5$ window median}

Fix the window radius $W=5$.  For each index $i$ let
\[
N_i \;=\; \bigl\{\, \mathrm{gap}_k :\; |k-i|\le 5,\; k\neq i,\;
0\le k<m \,\bigr\}
\]
and write $K=|N_i|$.  Sort $N_i$ increasingly as
$g_{(1)}\le\cdots\le g_{(K)}$.  The window median is the ordinary
odd-count median, or the midpoint of the two central values when
$K$ is even:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:med}
\mathrm{med}_i \;=\;
\begin{cases}
g_{(K/2+1/2)}, & K\text{ odd},\\[1mm]
\tfrac12\bigl(g_{(K/2)}+g_{(K/2+1)}\bigr), & K\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the interior of a long packing one has $K=10$ and $\mathrm{med}_i$
is the mean of the $5$th and $6$th order statistics.  At a left
endpoint with $m\ge 6$ one has $K=5$ and $\mathrm{med}_0$ is the
\emph{third-smallest} of five neighbour-gaps.  (The saturating atom
of \S\ref{sec:equality} is of this edge type.)

\begin{definition}[MED-CAP]
A packing satisfies \emph{MED-CAP} when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:medcap}
\mathrm{med}_i \;\le\; \tfrac83\,\mathrm{sep}_i
\qquad\text{for every }i=0,\dots,m-1.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The constant $8/3$ is not fitted.  It is the reciprocal of the
candidate floor $3/8$ for the normalised gaps
$g_i=\mathrm{gap}_i/\mathrm{med}_i$: the chain
$g_i\ge\mathrm{sep}_i/\mathrm{med}_i\ge 3/8$ closes pointwise if and
only if both the separation lower bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:sep} and
MED-CAP hold.

\section{What packing proves}

\begin{lemma}[SEP-SATZ]\label{lem:sep}
In every block packing,
$d_i \;=\; e_i + s_i \;\ge\; s_i$,
hence $\mathrm{gap}_i\ge\mathrm{sep}_i$ for every $i$.
Equality holds in the side identity if and only if $e_i=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From~\eqref{eq:centre},
\[
d_i
 \;=\;
\Bigl(L_{i+1}+\tfrac{n_{i+1}-1}{2}\Bigr)
-\Bigl(L_i+\tfrac{n_i-1}{2}\Bigr)
 \;=\;
L_{i+1}-L_i+\tfrac{n_{i+1}-n_i}{2}.
\]
Substitute $L_{i+1}=R_i+1+e_i=L_i+n_i+e_i$:
\[
d_i
 \;=\;
n_i+e_i+\tfrac{n_{i+1}-n_i}{2}
 \;=\;
e_i+\tfrac{n_i+n_{i+1}}{2}
 \;=\;
e_i+s_i.
\]
The gap is a minimum of one or two sides, the separation is the
same minimum of the corresponding $s$-values, and $e\ge 0$ gives
the inequality.
\end{proof}

Equivalently: a mean of $n$ distinct integers all lying on one
side of a cut sits at least $(n-1)/2$ away from the cut when the
integers are packed against it, and cannot sit closer.  Adjacent
blocks, being disjoint and ordered, are separated by a cut.

\begin{lemma}[tiling implies $\mathrm{gap}=\mathrm{sep}$]\label{lem:tile-gap}
If the packing is tiled ($e_i=0$ for all $i$), then
$d_i=s_i$ for every adjacent pair, and therefore
$\mathrm{gap}_i=\mathrm{sep}_i$ for every block.  In that case
MED-CAP is equivalent to
\[
\mathrm{med}_i \;\le\; \tfrac83\,\mathrm{gap}_i
\qquad\text{for every }i,
\]
a statement that refers only to the integer sequence $(n_i)_{i=0}^{m-1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sep} with $e_i=0$.
\end{proof}

Thus any proof of MED-CAP from packing axioms alone must either
use a positive lower bound on the unoccupied stretches (which
\emph{raises} sides and cannot help an upper bound on the median)
or constrain the length sequence $(n_i)$.  Empty stretches can
only make MED-CAP harder: they inflate neighbouring gaps while
leaving $\mathrm{sep}_i$ unchanged.  The useful packing axiom,
if there is one, is tiling plus regularity of $(n_i)$.

\section{What packing does not prove}\label{sec:c2}

\begin{lemma}[$C_2$ counterexample]\label{lem:c2}
The tiled packing of $m=12$ blocks with lengths
\[
n \;=\; (1,2,8,8,8,8,8,8,8,8,8,8)
\]
(so $e_i=0$ for all $i$) satisfies SEP-SATZ with equality on every
side, and violates MED-CAP at the two leftmost blocks, with ratio
$\mathrm{med}_i/\mathrm{sep}_i=16/3>8/3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sides are $d_0=(1+2)/2=3/2$ and $d_i=8$ for $i\ge 2$, with
$d_1=(2+8)/2=5$.  The gaps are therefore
$\mathrm{gap}_0=\mathrm{gap}_1=3/2$ and $\mathrm{gap}_i=8$ for
$i\ge 2$ except $\mathrm{gap}_2=\min(5,8)=5$.  At $i=0$ the
radius-$5$ window has five neighbours, with sorted gaps
$(3/2,\,5,\,8,\,8,\,8)$; the third-smallest is $8$.
The separation is $\mathrm{sep}_0=3/2$, and $8/(3/2)=16/3>8/3$.
The same median $8$ appears at $i=1$ against the same separation.
\end{proof}

A still shorter tiled witness is $n=(1,2,6,6,6,6)$: the five
neighbour-gaps at the left edge are $(3/2,\,4,\,6,\,6,\,6)$, the
third-smallest is $6$, and $6/(3/2)=4>8/3$.  Random tiled
compositions with $n_i\le 9$ and no Lipschitz constraint violate
MED-CAP in roughly one third of draws; imposing the observed
jump bound $|n_{i+1}-n_i|\le 12$ does not remove the violators.
Packings with a single large unoccupied stretch (the three
singletons at $\{0\}$, $\{1\}$, $\{10\}$) likewise violate, but
those are excluded the moment one assumes tiling.

Consequently MED-CAP is \emph{not} a theorem of disjoint contiguous
supports plus SEP-SATZ, and it is \emph{not} a theorem of tiled
compositions of an interval.  Any proof must use a further
constraint on $(n_i)$.

\section{The equality case}\label{sec:equality}

The constant $8/3$ is sharp.  Direct computation:

\begin{lemma}[saturating prefix]\label{lem:equality}
The tiled packing whose first six lengths are
\[
n \;=\; (1,\,2,\,6,\,5,\,4,\,4)
\]
has, at the left endpoint,
$\mathrm{gap}_0=\mathrm{sep}_0=3/2$ and $\mathrm{med}_0=4$, hence
\[
\frac{\mathrm{med}_0}{\mathrm{sep}_0} \;=\; \frac{4}{3/2} \;=\; \frac83.
\]
The same median and separation occur at $i=1$ (six neighbours, even
window: mean of the two central values).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Sides: $d_0=3/2$, $d_1=(2+6)/2=4$, $d_2=(6+5)/2=11/2$,
$d_3=(5+4)/2=9/2$, $d_4=4$.  Gaps:
$\mathrm{gap}_0=3/2$,
$\mathrm{gap}_1=\min(3/2,4)=3/2$,
$\mathrm{gap}_2=\min(4,11/2)=4$,
$\mathrm{gap}_3=\min(11/2,9/2)=9/2$,
$\mathrm{gap}_4=\min(9/2,4)=4$,
$\mathrm{gap}_5=4$.
The five neighbours of $i=0$ sort as
$(3/2,\,4,\,4,\,4,\,9/2)$; the third is $4$.
The six neighbours of $i=1$ sort as
$(3/2,\,4,\,4,\,4,\,4,\,9/2)$; the mean of the $3$rd and $4$th is
$4$.
\end{proof}

This prefix is realised by the folded packing of the Dirichlet
character $\chi_4$ at the admissible window of index $53$
($N=606$, $m=113$ blocks, $496$ occupied grid integers, tiling
exact, two atoms with $g_i=3/8$).  The bulk mode of that packing
is $n_i=4$ (and $n=4$ is the mode on every tested world,
\S\ref{sec:census}).  The arithmetic
\[
\frac83 \;=\; \frac{4}{3/2}
\]
is exactly bulk-mode pair-separation over the edge $(1,2)$-separation.
Replacing the tail $(5,4,4)$ by $(6,6,6)$ produces the short $C_2$
witness of \S\ref{sec:c2}: the third-smallest neighbour-gap jumps
from $4$ to $6$.  Saturation is therefore a \emph{one-sided} edge
phenomenon --- a short $(1,2)$ pair, a single overshoot to $n=6$,
and an immediate return to the bulk mode $4$.

\section{Tiling on the source packing}\label{sec:census}

The packings that motivated the inequality are the level-$2$
block decompositions of four families of folded discrete measures
(a prime-power comb, a second oversampling, and two Dirichlet
matchings), after an edge mask, on a finite list of windows.  Block
supports are the distinct integer images of the atoms under the
grid map $t=\arccos(x)\cdot(4N-2)/(2\pi)$, grouped by paired sign
runs in $x$-order.  The map is injective on the bulk up to the
quantisation bound $10^{-12}$; centres, sides, gaps and medians
are therefore exact rationals.

\begin{prop}[tiling census]\label{prop:tiling-census}
On $134$ windows comprising $15\,428$ blocks --- every admissible
window of both Dirichlet matchings ($42+42$), the $42$-rung core
ladder of the prime-power comb, and the eight sealed anchors of
the second family --- one has $e_i=0$ for every adjacent pair
($15\,294$ sides) and every block hull is solid (no unoccupied
integer inside any $I_i$).  Consequently $\mathrm{gap}_i=\mathrm{sep}_i$
identically on those $15\,428$ atoms, and MED-CAP holds with
$0$ violations, worst ratio exactly $8/3$ at the two atoms of
Lemma~\ref{lem:equality}.
\end{prop}

The same tiling identity is the reason the original $32\,728$-atom
census (all $181$ windows of the four families, including $47$
extension rungs of the prime-power comb not re-enumerated here)
recorded $0$ MED-CAP violations: under tiling the inequality
depends only on $(n_i)$, and that sequence, on the source, avoids
$C_2$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-c2}).  Tiling was not used in the
original census; it is the packing fact the census was pointing
at.

A structural reason for tiling, not used as a hypothesis below, is
available at the level of the grid map: after the edge mask the
surviving atoms occupy a convex $x$-interval, $\arccos$ is strictly
monotone, and empirically every integer of the resulting $t$-interval
is attained.  A vanishing theorem for the underlying trigonometric
symbol on that bulk interval would promote
Proposition~\ref{prop:tiling-census} from census to construction
algebra.  That promotion is not carried out here.

\section{The remaining invariant}\label{sec:obstruction}

Write $X_{\mathrm{tile}}$ for the assertion that the packing is
tiled and hull-solid.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:tile-gap},
\[
X_{\mathrm{tile}}\;+\;\text{MED-CAP}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
X_{\mathrm{tile}}\;+\;X_n,
\]
where $X_n$ is the assertion that the length sequence $(n_i)$ is
$(8/3)$-median-regular in the sense of~\eqref{eq:medcap} with
$\mathrm{sep}_i=\mathrm{gap}_i$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:c2} says $X_n$ is
not true of every composition of an interval.

Two sufficient conditions that \emph{would} imply $X_n$ both fail
on the source, or fail to be sufficient:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Local oscillation of gaps.}
If $\max/\min$ of the gaps in every radius-$5$ window were at most
$8/3$, MED-CAP would follow at once (the window median cannot
exceed the window maximum).  At the saturating prefix of
Lemma~\ref{lem:equality} one has $\max/\min=(9/2)/(3/2)=3>8/3$.
The bound on the \emph{maximum} is too crude; MED-CAP bounds an
order statistic.
\item \emph{Interior restriction $n_i\in\{3,4,5\}$.}
Then every interior side lies in $[3,5]$ and every interior ratio
is at most $5/3<8/3$.  On the source this fails at several hundred
interior blocks per family (mode $n=4$ is only about $58$~percent;
$n=2$ occurs in the interior, especially on the second family).
Those interior $n=2$ blocks sit next to $n=4$ neighbours, so
$\mathrm{sep}=3$ and the budget $(8/3)\cdot 3=8$ is generous ---
they are not $C_2$.
\item \emph{Sign-run pairing.}
On the source, blocks are paired consecutive sign-runs of a
$\pm$ colouring of the tiled interval (an odd tail is a block by
itself).  That algebra yields $n=1$ only as an unpaired singleton
tail and $n=2$ as a pair of singleton runs, which is compatible
with the saturating prefix.  It does \emph{not} forbid $C_2$: the
colouring ``three singleton runs, then a run of length $8$'' is a
legal sign sequence and realises $n=(1,2,8,\dots)$ at the odd-tail
edge.
\end{itemize}

\begin{prop}[no $C_2$ on the source]\label{prop:no-c2}
Among the $15\,428$ atoms of Proposition~\ref{prop:tiling-census}
there are $114$ blocks with $n_i\le 2$ that see some $n_j\ge 7$
inside their radius-$5$ window.  None of them violates MED-CAP
(worst such ratio $12/5<8/3$, at the left edge of the $\chi_3$
window of index $20$, prefix $(1,4,8,4,8,4)$, separation $5/2$).
The unique atoms that saturate $8/3$ are the two of
Lemma~\ref{lem:equality}.  Adjacent $(n,n')=(1,2)$ with a later
window value $\ge 8$ does not occur.
\end{prop}

The named remainder is therefore:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Invariant $X_n$.}\enspace
The length sequence of the tiled packing does not contain, at any
small-separation locus ($\mathrm{sep}_i=3/2$, i.e.\ an adjacent
$(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$ pair, or $\mathrm{sep}_i=1$ from two adjacent
singletons), a radius-$5$ window whose third-smallest neighbour-gap
(edge) or mean of the $5$th and $6$th (interior) exceeds
$(8/3)\,\mathrm{sep}_i$.  Equivalently: after an edge $(1,2)$ pair,
an overshoot to $n=6$ is permitted only if the sequence returns to
the bulk mode $4$ fast enough that at most two of the five
neighbour-gaps exceed $4$; an overshoot to $n\ge 7$ at that locus
is forbidden.

\medskip
$X_n$ is a property of the \emph{sign field} of the folded measure
(or of whatever mechanism produces the observed $n$-histogram,
mode $4$, geometric-like run lengths), not of interval packing.
A proof of $X_n$ is a proof of MED-CAP on every tiled packing that
the construction produces.  A counterexample to $X_n$ on a source
window would be a MED-CAP violation, and none is known.

\section{T1 preview}\label{sec:t1}

Assume MED-CAP.  Then $g_i=\mathrm{gap}_i/\mathrm{med}_i\ge 3/8$
pointwise, so the dyadic packing count $S_r=\sum_{g_i\le 2^{-r}}q_i$
vanishes for every $r\ge 2$ (since $2^{-2}<3/8$), and the cubic
mass of the block profile satisfies
\[
M_3
 \;\le\;
\Bigl(\tfrac83\Bigr)^2 C_K^2 \frac{(\log m)^2}{m^2}
\]
once the localisation bound $(m q_i/\log m)\,g_i\le C_K$ is granted.
Two logarithms and the second Carleson constant drop.  What does
\emph{not} drop is $C_K$ itself, and the present attempt explains
why a packing proof of MED-CAP would not have produced it.

MED-CAP, even after the tiling reduction, is an order-statistic
bound on the \emph{length sequence} of blocks, independent of the
block masses $q_i$.  The T1 bound is a statement about those
masses relative to the same gaps.  A purely geometric MED-CAP
(which this note refutes) would have suggested that T1 might also
be local packing; the obstruction $X_n$ being a sign-field
constraint says the opposite.  The masses $q_i$ see the same sign
field through the defect measure, but they see it as an amplitude,
not as a run length.  Proving $X_n$ does not create a
family-uniform $C_K$, and the r358 record that the per-family
freeze maximum fails on the test set (ceiling $23.70$ against
freeze $4.91$) stands unchanged.  The single remaining
quantitative object of the terminal lane, after MED-CAP, is that
T1 constant rule --- to be addressed on its own terms, not as a
corollary of the median cap.

Finite identities and a named combinatorial remainder on a finite
family of explicit packings.  No statement about zeros of
$\zeta(s)$, in either direction.  No RH claim.
